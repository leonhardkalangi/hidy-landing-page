How to password protect a PDF on iPhone

Updated 27 October 2026 · By Hidy

Open the PDF in the Files app, tap More (…), tap Lock, enter the password twice, then tap Apply. Keep an unlocked copy first, because the lock is meant to stop edits and deletion.

Quick answer
  • Keep an unlocked copy: open the PDF, tap More (…) > Duplicate.
  • Tap More (…) > Lock, enter the password twice, tap Apply.
  • The lock is documented to stop editing and deleting, not opening.

What does the Files lock actually do?

Apple's Files instructions say the lock is there to prevent others from editing or deleting the PDF. They do not say it stops someone from opening or viewing the file. That is the honest limit, and it matters if your goal is privacy rather than tamper protection.

So: use the lock to protect a document from being changed or removed. Do not assume it turns the PDF into a sealed file that nobody can read.

Step by step: lock a PDF in Files

  1. Open the Files app and open the PDF.
  2. Tap More (…) and tap Duplicate. This keeps an unlocked copy before you lock anything.
  3. Tap More (…) next to the filename, then tap Lock.
  4. Enter the same password in Password and Verify. The Require Password toggle turns on automatically.
  5. Tap Apply (the checkmark) to lock the PDF, or Cancel (×) to discard the change.

Apple's page does not specify a minimum password length, a character set, a retry limit or a recovery option. Choose a long password you can remember, and store it somewhere separate from the PDF.

Why keep an unlocked copy first?

Because the lock is easy to forget and Apple's instructions do not describe a recovery path. If you lock the only copy and lose the password, you may be stuck with a file you cannot edit or delete.

Duplicate the PDF before locking it, then move the unlocked copy somewhere you control. If you are keeping passport or ID scans, see keeping passport and ID scans on your iPhone.

How to unlock a PDF again

Apple's Files instructions do not describe a step for turning the lock off. That is why the unlocked copy you made before locking matters: it is your way back to an editable file.

Password-protecting a document in Pages

If the document is still in Pages, Apple documents a separate password option there. This is a different flow from locking a PDF in Files, and it applies to Pages documents rather than finished PDFs.

For a PDF you already exported, the Files lock above is the documented iPhone route.

What the Files lock does not cover

  • Opening or viewing. Apple's instructions describe the lock as stopping edits and deletion, not reading.
  • Other copies. A duplicate, an emailed copy or a copy in another app is not affected by the lock on this file.
  • The rest of your iPhone. The lock protects one PDF. It does not lock the Files app, your photo library or your notes.
  • Password recovery. Apple's page does not describe one.

Does locking a PDF stop someone from opening it?

Apple's Files instructions describe the lock as preventing others from editing or deleting the PDF. They do not say it stops someone from opening or viewing it.

Can I undo the lock?

Apple's Files instructions do not describe a step for turning the lock off. Keep the unlocked copy you made before locking.

What if I forget the PDF password?

Apple's page does not describe a recovery option. Keep the unlocked copy you made before locking.

Is the password stored in iCloud?

Apple's Files instructions do not say where the password is stored. Treat it as something only you should know.
