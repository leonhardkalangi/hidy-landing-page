iPhone Safari Private Browsing: What It Hides and What It Doesn't

Updated 26 November 2026 · By Hidy

Open Safari, tap the More button (…), then New Private Tab. Private Browsing stops Safari from saving your history, searches and AutoFill details on that iPhone. It does not hide your activity from your network, your employer or your carrier.

Quick answer
  • Open: Safari > More (…) > New Private Tab.
  • Lock tabs: Settings > Apps > Safari > Require Face ID to Unlock Private Browsing.
  • Not covered: your network, employer, carrier or the sites you visit.

How do I open Private Browsing in Safari on iPhone?

Open Safari, tap the More button (…) and choose New Private Tab.

There is a second route if you prefer tab groups. Tap the Tabs button, tap Private, then tap Done. You land in the same private space.

Everything you open from there stays in that private session until you close the tabs.

What does Private Browsing actually stop?

Apple is specific about this. Safari does not remember the pages you visit, your search history or your AutoFill information while you are in a private tab.

  • No entry in Safari History for those pages.
  • No saved searches from the private address bar.
  • No AutoFill details remembered from forms you fill in.

That is the whole promise. It is about what Safari writes down on the iPhone, not about who can watch the connection.

Do private tabs sync to my iPad or Mac?

No. Apple says private tabs do not appear in iPhone History or in the tab list on your other Apple devices. They are not shared across devices, even when every device uses the same Apple Account.

This is one of the more useful parts of the feature. A tab you opened privately on your iPhone will not surface on a shared family iPad later.

Can I lock private tabs behind Face ID?

Yes, and it is worth turning on. Private tabs lock when the iPhone locks. To reopen them you authenticate with Face ID, Touch ID or the device passcode.

You can make that lock explicit:

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Go to Apps > Safari.
  3. Turn on Require Face ID to Unlock Private Browsing, or the Touch ID or Passcode version your iPhone supports.

After that, handing someone an unlocked phone does not hand them your private tabs.

Do Safari extensions run in private tabs?

Apple says extensions that have access to browsing data are turned off automatically in Private Browsing. If you installed a content blocker or a reading tool that reads pages, expect it to be inactive there.

That cuts both ways. Fewer extensions see your private pages, and some tools you rely on simply will not work.

Can I use a different search engine for private tabs only?

Yes. In Settings > Apps > Safari, turn off Also Use in Private Browsing, then tap Private Search Engine and pick one.

This lets you keep, say, Google for everyday browsing and a different engine for private tabs. The choice is per mode, not per tab.

Which Safari privacy settings should I check?

Apple documents three settings under Settings > Apps > Safari that you can turn on or off:

SettingWhat it is for
Prevent Cross-Site TrackingNamed by Apple as a Safari privacy setting.
Hide IP AddressNamed by Apple as a Safari privacy setting.
Fraudulent Website WarningNamed by Apple as a Safari privacy setting.

These apply to Safari generally, not only to private tabs. Turning them on is a separate decision from using Private Browsing.

What Private Browsing does not hide

This is the part people get wrong, so it deserves its own section.

  • Your network. The Wi-Fi operator, your mobile carrier and anyone running the network can see which sites your iPhone connects to.
  • Your employer. On a work-managed iPhone or a work network, traffic may be inspected regardless of which Safari mode you use.
  • The websites. Sites still see your request, and they can still use cookies and scripts within that private session.
  • Downloads and files. Anything you download lands in Files or Photos like any other download.
  • Screenshots and screen recording. Nothing in Safari stops another person or another app from capturing what is on screen.

Private Browsing is a history setting. It is not a network shield and not a vault.

Limits worth knowing before you rely on it

Private tabs are convenient, but they are not a place to keep something you would be harmed by losing control of.

Anyone holding your unlocked iPhone, with the lock setting off, can open Safari and see the private tabs still open. The tabs persist until you close them, so a session you forgot about is a session someone else can read.

If you are checking something sensitive, close the private tabs when you finish. If you are signing in somewhere private, sign out at the end rather than trusting the tab to disappear.

And if the thing you are protecting is a file rather than a web page, Safari is the wrong tool. A downloaded PDF or a saved image sits in Files or Photos, readable by anyone who opens those apps.

Quick checklist

  1. Open private tabs with More (…) > New Private Tab.
  2. Turn on Require Face ID to Unlock Private Browsing in Settings.
  3. Pick a separate private search engine if you want one.
  4. Check Prevent Cross-Site Tracking and Hide IP Address are on.
  5. Close private tabs when you are done, and sign out of anything you signed into.

Frequently asked questions

Does Private Browsing hide my activity from my internet provider?

No. Apple's description covers what Safari saves on the device. Your carrier, Wi-Fi operator and the sites you visit can still see the connection.

Do private tabs show up on my other Apple devices?

No. Apple says private tabs don't appear in iPhone History or in the tab list on your other devices, even with the same Apple Account.

Can someone open my private tabs if they have my iPhone?

Private tabs lock when the iPhone locks. You can also require Face ID, Touch ID or the passcode before Safari shows them.

Do Safari extensions work in Private Browsing?

Apple says extensions with access to browsing data are turned off automatically in Private Browsing.

Does Private Browsing stop tracking by the websites themselves?

Not by itself. Safari has separate settings for cross-site tracking and IP address hiding, and those apply to normal browsing too.
