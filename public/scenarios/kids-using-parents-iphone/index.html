Kids using your iPhone: what to lock down first

Updated 1 November 2026 · By Hidy

Hand your iPhone to a child and the risk is not a virus. It is a camera roll that opens on the last photo you viewed, and a child who swipes. Set up Guided Access or Screen Time for the handoff, and keep private files in an encrypted vault the child cannot reach.

Quick answer
  • Regular use: Screen Time with Content & Privacy Restrictions limits apps, purchases and websites.
  • Private files: move them into an encrypted vault, not the Hidden album.

What actually goes wrong when a child uses your iPhone

Most parents worry about the wrong thing. A child is unlikely to install malware. A child is very likely to open Photos, swipe through the camera roll, and stop on something private. They may also tap a purchase, open a browser, or send a message from your account.

So the job splits in two. First, limit what the child can open during the handoff. Second, keep the files you never want opened in a place the child cannot reach at all, even if they find the app.

Quick handoff: lock the iPhone to one app

Guided Access keeps the iPhone in a single app until you end it. Apple documents it at support.apple.com/en-us/111795.

  1. Open the app you want the child to use, such as a game or a video app.
  2. Use Guided Access to lock the iPhone to that app.
  3. Hand over the iPhone. The child stays in that app.

This is the fastest option for a five-minute handoff.

Regular use: Screen Time and Content & Privacy Restrictions

If a child uses your iPhone often, or has their own device tied to your account, Screen Time is the longer-term answer. Apple's setup guide is at support.apple.com/en-us/108806, and the restrictions page is at support.apple.com/en-gb/105121.

Start with Family Sharing on your iPhone: Settings > [your name] > Family, then follow the onscreen instructions. Add or create the child's Apple Account there. Then open Settings > Family > [child's name] > Screen Time and follow the prompts.

From Content & Privacy Restrictions you can:

  • Turn on Ask to Buy so purchases need your approval.
  • Set App Installations & Purchases to Don't Allow.
  • Choose which built-in apps appear under Allowed Apps & Features. Disabled apps are hidden, not deleted.
  • Set age ratings under Allowed Store Content.
  • Filter websites with Limit Adult Websites or Ask to Browse.
  • Set daily time allowances and schedules, such as before school or during school.

These controls apply to the child's account. They are not a lock on your own camera roll when you hand over your unlocked phone. For that, use Guided Access, or keep the private files somewhere else.

The camera roll problem

If you were looking at something private, that is what a child may see first. Apple's Hidden album helps a little: on iOS 16 and later it is locked by default, and it opens with Face ID, Touch ID or the passcode. Apple documents this at support.apple.com/guide/iphone/delete-or-hide-photos-and-videos-iphb4defbde9/ios.

But the Hidden album still lives inside Photos.

If you want a real boundary, move the files out of Photos and into an encrypted vault. In Hidy Vault, a vault opens only when you draw its pattern on a 5×5 grid. A child who taps the icon sees a lock screen. The app also locks as soon as it goes to the background, so a quick app switch does not leave it open.

What to move, and how

  1. Import the photos and videos you want out of the camera roll into a vault.
  2. Let Hidy offer to delete the originals from Photos. iOS asks you to confirm, and the originals stay in Recently Deleted for up to 30 days.
  3. Turn off the delete offer in Settings if you would rather keep the originals in Photos.
  4. Use Hidy's own camera for new private photos. Those files save straight into the vault and never reach the Photos library.

Import quality is set to Optimized by default. That makes smaller files and removes location data from photos and videos. Choose Original if you want the file kept exactly as it is.

What about the child's own photos?

If the child takes photos on your iPhone, those land in your camera roll. That is fine for most families. The point of the vault is to separate your private files from the shared space, not to hide the child's work.

For a shared family iPad or a shared Apple Account, the same logic applies. Keep private files in a vault with its own pattern, and let the shared device hold only what everyone may see.

Limits you should know

  • Guided Access and Screen Time are not encryption. They limit what a child can open, not what someone determined can find.
  • Hidy has no Face ID or Touch ID unlock. You draw a pattern each time.
  • Hidy cannot reset a forgotten pattern. Set up the 12-word recovery phrase for each vault you care about.
  • An open vault is readable on screen. Close the app when you hand the phone over.
  • Sharing a file out of Hidy gives the recipient a readable copy. Do not share a private file into a chat a child can open.
  • Hidy is coming to the App Store and is not yet available there.

None of this makes a phone childproof. It makes the private parts private, which is the part you can actually control.

Frequently asked questions

Can I lock my iPhone to one app before handing it over?

Yes. Guided Access locks the iPhone to a single app. Apple documents it at support.apple.com/en-us/111795.

Can Screen Time stop a child from installing apps?

Yes. Under Content & Privacy Restrictions you can set App Installations & Purchases to Don't Allow. Apple documents this at support.apple.com/en-gb/105121. It applies to the child's account, not to a phone you hand over unlocked.

What if my child already saw a private photo?

Move the file into an encrypted vault now, and check the Hidden album and Recently Deleted. Apple keeps Recently Deleted items for 30 days, so you can still remove something you no longer want on the phone.

Is a vault app safe if my child finds the icon?

Only if the vault itself is locked. In Hidy Vault, nothing opens without a vault's pattern, and the app locks as soon as it goes to the background. A child who taps the icon sees a lock screen, not your files.
