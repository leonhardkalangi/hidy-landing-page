How to show one photo without letting someone swipe

Updated 22 October 2026 · By Hidy

iOS has no "show only this photo" mode in Photos. The safest options are to not hand over your library at all: share the photo, or open it from a place that contains nothing else.

Practical answer
  • Best: send it. AirDrop or Messages puts the photo on their screen, not your library in their hands.
  • Handing over the phone: open the photo from a vault that holds only that photo. Swiping shows nothing else.
  • Kids and long handovers: use Guided Access to lock the iPhone to one app.

The three ways

1. Send it instead

AirDrop, Messages or any chat app shows the photo on their device. Nothing of yours is in their hands. This is the only option with zero risk of extra swiping, but it does give them a copy.

2. Open it from a one-photo vault

Make a vault just for "photos I show people". Put the photo there, open it, hand over the phone. Swiping left and right only moves between photos in that vault.

  1. In Hidy Vault, draw a new pattern you'll use only for this. It opens an empty vault.
  2. Import the photo (or several you often show, like your pets or your flat).
  3. Open the photo and hand over the phone. When they give it back, leave the app and it locks.

3. Guided Access

Guided Access in Accessibility settings locks the iPhone to one app until you enter a passcode. It's built for kids and kiosks. In Photos, it still allows swiping through the whole library, so combine it with option 2 if that matters (Apple: Use Guided Access).

What not to rely on

  • "I'll just hold the phone." One tap on their side and you're scrolling together.
  • A shared album. It sends copies to everyone in it and keeps them in sync.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a way to stop swiping in Photos?

Not within the Photos app. Guided Access can keep someone inside one app, but they can still swipe between photos in that app.

Does Guided Access stop them seeing other photos?

It keeps them in the app you started. In Photos, that still means your whole library. It's more useful for handing a child a game or video.

Will the other person know it's a vault?

They'll see an app with one photo in it. Hidy locks as soon as you leave it, so take the phone back, and the vault is closed.
