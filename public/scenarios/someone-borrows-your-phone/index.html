Someone wants to borrow your phone

Updated 22 October 2026 · By Hidy

Lending a phone for a call or a map takes seconds. What they can open in those seconds is up to you. A few settings now make the next handover a non-event.

Practical answer
  • For one app: Guided Access keeps them inside it until you enter a passcode.
  • Lock the apps that matter with Require Face ID (iOS 18 and later).
  • Keep the most private files in a vault that locks when you leave it.

What a borrowed phone exposes

An unlocked iPhone opens to everything: Photos, Messages, mail, notes, banking notifications. Most borrowers never look. The problem is the one time someone does, or a notification pops up while they're holding it.

Three layers

  1. Guided Access for short handovers. Turn it on in Settings > Accessibility > Guided Access, then triple-click the side button in the app you want to allow. They stay in that app (Apple).
  2. Lock sensitive apps. Touch and hold Photos, Messages or Files, then Require Face ID. Apple also keeps a locked app's content out of search and notification previews (Apple).
  3. A vault for what matters most. Hidy Vault locks the moment you leave it, and its pattern isn't your passcode.

Checklist

  • Guided Access turned on, with its own passcode.
  • Photos and Files set to Require Face ID.
  • Private photos moved out of the library into a vault.
  • Notification previews set to "When Unlocked" in Settings > Notifications.

Frequently asked questions

Does Require Face ID stop someone using my phone?

It stops them opening that specific app without Face ID or your passcode. Everything else stays open.

Is Guided Access easy to undo?

They'd need your Guided Access passcode or Face ID to leave the app.

Do I need to lock Hidy before lending the phone?

Hidy locks as soon as it goes to the background. As long as you've left the app, the vault is locked.
