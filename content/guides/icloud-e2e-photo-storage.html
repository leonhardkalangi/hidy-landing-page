Is iCloud Photos end-to-end encrypted?

Not by default. Apple's standard data protection is the default for iCloud. Your data is encrypted, but Apple holds the keys in its data centers so it can help with recovery. Under that default, Apple end-to-end encrypts only certain data.

Advanced Data Protection is the optional setting that changes this. Apple says it end-to-end encrypts most iCloud data, including iCloud Backup, Photos and Notes. Only your trusted devices hold the keys, and not even Apple can access it.

So the honest answer has two parts. iCloud Photos is encrypted. One account-wide setting decides whether it is end-to-end encrypted.

What Advanced Data Protection changes

Advanced Data Protection applies to the whole account, not to one app. Apple lists these effects:

  • Most iCloud data becomes end-to-end encrypted, including iCloud Backup, Photos and Notes.
  • Only trusted devices hold the keys.
  • Apple disables web access at iCloud.com by default.

Turn it on in Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Advanced Data Protection > Turn on. Apple notes that Shared Albums in Photos and "anyone with the link" sharing stay outside end-to-end encryption even with Advanced Data Protection on. iCloud Shared Photo Library is end-to-end encrypted if every participant uses Advanced Data Protection.

What stays outside end-to-end encryption

Even with Advanced Data Protection on, some photo features fall outside it:

FeatureEnd-to-end encrypted with ADP?
Your iCloud Photos libraryYes
iCloud Backup (including app data)Yes
Shared AlbumsNo
"Anyone with the link" sharingNo
iCloud Shared Photo LibraryYes, if all participants use ADP

That last row matters for families. One member without Advanced Data Protection changes the answer for the shared library.

How to turn on Advanced Data Protection

  1. Open Settings on your iPhone.
  2. Tap your name, then iCloud.
  3. Tap Advanced Data Protection.
  4. Tap Turn on and follow the onscreen steps.

Apple says the setting applies to the whole account. Set it up when you have time to read the recovery steps. Losing access to your trusted devices and recovery methods matters more once the keys are only on your devices.

What about iCloud Photos itself?

iCloud Photos keeps every photo and video in iCloud at full resolution. Changes on one device appear on the others. Deleting a photo on one device deletes it everywhere iCloud Photos is used, and Recently Deleted keeps it 30 days.

Apple's iCloud Photos pages describe photos as securely stored. They do not document a separate end-to-end encryption switch inside the Photos app. If you go looking for one, you will not find it. The setting lives in iCloud settings.

Apple adds one more detail: iCloud Photos is not duplicated in iCloud Backup. Apple recommends keeping your own backup copies of the library. Without iCloud Photos, the Photos library is part of iCloud Backup.

When you want photos out of iCloud entirely

Advanced Data Protection is the strongest iCloud setting Apple offers. It is still iCloud. If the goal is photos that never sit in a cloud account at all, the answer is local storage.

Hidy Vault encrypts photos and videos on the iPhone with AES-256-GCM before anything else happens. No Hidy server stores vaults. iCloud backup is optional per vault, needs the 12-word recovery phrase first, and uploads only what the iPhone already encrypted, into your own iCloud. Hidy is coming to the App Store.

Limits worth knowing

  • Advanced Data Protection is account-wide. You cannot apply it to Photos alone.
  • Shared Albums stay outside end-to-end encryption even with it on.
  • Apple can still help with recovery under standard data protection, which is exactly the trade-off.
  • Hidy has no independent cryptographic audit yet. An open vault is readable on screen while it is open.
  • Someone examining Hidy's storage can see approximate file sizes and the number of vault folders, not contents or names.

Pick based on the threat you care about. For most people, Advanced Data Protection plus a strong device passcode covers the iCloud side. For photos you would not want in any account, a local encrypted vault is the simpler answer.
