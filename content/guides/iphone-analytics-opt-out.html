Where is the iPhone analytics setting?

Open Settings, tap Privacy & Security, then tap Analytics or Analytics & Improvements. The label depends on your iOS version. At the top you will find Share iPhone & Watch Analytics.

Turn that switch off. Apple documents this path for iOS 10.3 and later, so it applies to iOS 18 and later too.

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap Privacy & Security.
  3. Tap Analytics (or Analytics & Improvements).
  4. Turn off Share iPhone & Watch Analytics.

That is the whole opt-out. There is no account to close and no form to submit.

What does Apple actually collect?

Apple says the analytics, diagnostic and usage information it collects is anonymous. That is Apple's own description on its support page.

The same page covers the setting that shares some of this data with app developers. Apple documents Share With App Developers only for iOS 8 and iOS 9. It is not listed as an iOS 18-or-later setting on that page, so do not expect to find it on a current iPhone.

If you want to read Apple's wording yourself, the source link is at the bottom of this page.

What the switch does not turn off

This is where most guides oversell the setting. Turning off analytics sharing is one switch, not a privacy mode.

  • App permissions. Apps still ask for access to Photos, location and the microphone. You manage those in Settings > Privacy & Security. See which apps can see your photos.
  • iCloud sync. Photos, backups and app data still sync through your Apple Account. That is a separate system with its own settings.
  • Apple Intelligence features. Those have their own access controls, documented separately by Apple.
  • App tracking. Third-party apps track you under their own rules and their own permission prompts.

So the analytics switch answers one question: does my iPhone send this diagnostic data to Apple? It does not answer "is my phone private?"

Does opting out have a time limit or a catch?

Apple's support page states no time, quantity or other limit for opting out, and none for the Share iPhone & Watch Analytics setting itself.

The page also does not describe deleting analytics that were already sent. It covers the setting, not a data-removal request.

If the switch is already off, nothing more is needed.

Analytics is not the same as photo privacy

People often search for this setting after a photo worry. The two are unrelated. Analytics sharing is about diagnostic data going to Apple. Photo privacy is about who can open your library on the phone itself.

Those are different problems with different fixes:

ConcernWhere it is handled
Diagnostic data sent to AppleSettings > Privacy & Security > Analytics
Apps reading your librarySettings > Privacy & Security > Photos
Someone opening your phonePasscode, Face ID, and what you keep on the device
Photos you would rather not store in the libraryA separate encrypted store, such as a vault app

If your actual worry is a person scrolling your camera roll, the analytics switch will not help. Start with hiding photos on iPhone or locking photos.

Where a vault fits, and where it does not

A vault app is for the photos and files you do not want sitting in the Photos library at all. Hidy Vault encrypts file contents, file names, thumbnails, folders and notes with AES-256-GCM. The key comes from a pattern you draw on a 5×5 grid plus a device secret held in the iPhone Keychain.

Two honest points. First, Hidy is not yet on the App Store; it is coming to the App Store. Second, Hidy has no independent cryptographic audit yet. The encryption is described in the app and on this site, not certified by an outside firm.

Hidy also has no analytics or advertising SDK and no server that stores vaults. That is a product design choice, not a claim that the rest of iOS stops collecting data.

Limits of this guide

  • We describe only what Apple documents on its own support pages. Menu labels can differ between iOS versions.
  • We cannot tell you what Apple does with analytics data internally. Apple's page says anonymous; we repeat that and nothing more.
  • Turning off analytics does not make your iPhone anonymous to your carrier, your network or the apps you install.
  • This page is not legal or security advice for a specific threat. If someone is targeting you, talk to a local support service.

Do the switch, then look at the settings that actually match your worry. Analytics is a small piece of a larger picture.
