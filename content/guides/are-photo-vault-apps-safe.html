The 7 checks

  1. Encrypted on the device? Look for a clear statement that files are encrypted on the phone. "Password protected" alone often means a lock screen in front of readable files.
  2. Where does the key come from? The best answer: from something only you know, like your code or pattern, combined with a device secret.
  3. Can the company reset your access? An email reset is convenient, and it means someone other than you can get in.
  4. How do backups work? Are they encrypted with a key only you hold? Are they in the free version? What happens if you lose the phone?
  5. Ads and trackers? Read the App Privacy section on the App Store page: "Data Used to Track You" and "Data Linked to You".
  6. Account required? An account isn't bad by itself, but it is one more place your data and identity are linked.
  7. Does it admit limits? Trustworthy apps say what they don't protect. Be wary of "unhackable" and "100% secure".

Common red flags

  • A free vault full of ads.
  • No mention of encryption, or encryption only for a paid cloud.
  • "Forgot your PIN? We'll email you a reset."
  • Claims nobody can verify, like "military-grade" with no details.
