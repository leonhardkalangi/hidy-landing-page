What the passcode opens

  • The Hidden album. It unlocks with Face ID, Touch ID or the passcode.
  • Apps you locked with Require Face ID. The passcode is the fallback.
  • Notes locked with the device passcode. A separate notes password is the exception.
  • Your Apple Account settings, including where your photos sync.

Where the real problem is

Almost every built-in lock on iPhone falls back to the same passcode. That's convenient for you and for anyone you've told it to. To keep something private from a person who knows the passcode, it has to sit behind a different secret.

Keep one space of your own

  1. Install Hidy Vault and create a vault with a pattern you've never used anywhere else.
  2. Move in what's yours: photos, screenshots, documents. Delete the originals and empty Recently Deleted.
  3. Create the vault's recovery phrase and keep it somewhere they don't look.
  4. Optional (Pro): switch the icon to Notes or Weather, or hide the app with iOS's Hide and Require Face ID.

Setup checklist

  • A vault pattern that isn't related to your passcode or birthdays.
  • Recently Deleted emptied.
  • Recovery phrase stored outside the phone.
  • If you share an Apple Account or iCloud Photos, read photos on a shared Apple Account.

If this is about safety

If you're worried about being monitored or controlled, Apple's Personal Safety User Guide explains Safety Check, which reviews and resets what you share. Changing what's on your phone can be noticed, so think about your safety first.
